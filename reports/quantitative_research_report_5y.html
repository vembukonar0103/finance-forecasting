1. Executive Summary

Objective: This study evaluates six forecasting models—two foundation models (Google TimesFM 2.5-200M and Kronos), two traditional ML baselines (Ridge Regression with and without technical indicators), and two ensemble strategies (Weighted and Confidence-based)—across five diverse assets (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, SPY, BTC-USD) over a rigorous 5-year walk-forward validation framework with 30-day rolling forecast horizons.

Key Findings:

Best Forecasting Model: TimesFM consistently achieved the lowest MAE and RMSE across most assets, with directional accuracy typically above 50%. BaseML (Ridge without technical indicators) also demonstrated competitive forecast accuracy on select assets.

Best Trading Model: BaseML (Ridge with lag features only) generated the highest portfolio CAGR among all models, driven by an exceptional +67.13% CAGR on NVDA. However, no model consistently outperformed the buy-and-hold benchmark across all assets.

Technical Indicators: Adding technical indicators (BaseML_Technical) degraded both forecast accuracy and trading performance relative to the lag-only BaseML baseline, suggesting overfitting and multicollinearity in the expanded feature set.

Ensembles: Neither the Weighted nor Confidence Ensemble improved upon the individual foundation models. The Confidence Ensemble, in particular, suffered from over-weighting of the weaker model in certain regimes.

Recommendation: TimesFM is recommended as the primary forecasting engine for production deployment due to its superior accuracy, probabilistic output, and sub-second inference speed. Trading signals require further refinement before live deployment.

2. Methodology

2.1 Historical Data

Five years of daily price data were sourced via yfinance for each asset. Equity tickers (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, SPY) yielded approximately 1,300 business-day observations; BTC-USD yielded approximately 1,825 calendar-day observations due to 24/7 trading. A 5-year history was chosen over the initial 3-year period because a 500-day context warmup window is required before the first test prediction can begin. With 3 years (~750 rows for equities), only ~250 testable rows remained—fewer than one year of walk-forward coverage. Extending to 5 years provides ~800 testable equity rows (≈3.2 years) and ~1,325 testable BTC-USD rows (≈3.6 years), ensuring statistically meaningful evaluation across multiple market regimes.

2.2 Walk-Forward Validation

Walk-forward validation is a time-series cross-validation technique that simulates how a model would perform in production by retraining and forecasting on sequentially advancing windows of data.

Rolling Windows: Starting from row 500, non-overlapping 30-day test windows advance sequentially through the dataset. Each window uses all prior data as context (expanding window for context, fixed 30-day test).

Train / Validation / Test Split: For each window: Context = all rows up to the split point; Validation = last 60 days of context (used for hyperparameter tuning and ensemble weighting); Test = next 30 days (strictly out-of-sample).

No Look-Ahead Bias: A formal verification function confirms that features computed on truncated context data exactly match those computed on the full dataset up to the same date, ensuring no future information leaks into the model.

Superiority Over Single Split: Unlike a single train-test split, walk-forward validation evaluates performance across ~25-44 independent windows spanning bull markets, bear markets, and varying volatility regimes, yielding robust and statistically representative results.

Windows Generated: Equities: ~25-27 windows (≈800 test days); BTC-USD: ~44 windows (≈1,325 test days).

2.3 Feature Engineering

The feature engineering pipeline computes 25 technical indicators using TA-Lib, organized into four categories:

Trend Indicators

SMA (10, 20, 50, 200-day): Simple Moving Average smooths price noise by averaging closing prices over a lookback window. It identifies trend direction (price above SMA = bullish) and acts as dynamic support/resistance. Provides ML models with a denoised trend signal that reduces overfitting to daily fluctuations.

EMA (10, 20, 50, 200-day): Exponential Moving Average assigns exponentially decaying weights to recent observations, making it more responsive to recent price changes than SMA. Captures trend momentum shifts earlier, improving forecasting of trend reversals and breakouts.

Momentum Indicators

RSI (14-day): Relative Strength Index measures the velocity and magnitude of price movements on a 0-100 scale. Values above 70 indicate overbought conditions; below 30 indicate oversold. Helps models identify mean-reversion opportunities.

MACD: Moving Average Convergence Divergence tracks the relationship between two EMAs (12-day and 26-day). The MACD line, signal line, and histogram capture momentum changes and potential trend reversals before they appear in price.

CCI (14-day): Commodity Channel Index measures deviation of price from its statistical mean. Extreme values suggest the asset is overbought or oversold relative to its typical price range, providing cyclical reversal signals.

Williams %R (14-day): Measures the current close relative to the highest high over the lookback period. Ranges from -100 to 0; values near -100 indicate oversold conditions. Complements RSI with a different mathematical perspective on momentum.

Stochastic Oscillator (Slow %K, %D): Compares the closing price to the price range over a given period. Crossovers between %K and %D lines generate buy/sell signals. Effective in range-bound markets for identifying reversal points.

Volatility Indicators

ATR (14-day): Average True Range measures market volatility by decomposing the entire range of an asset's price for the period. Helps models calibrate position sizing and set appropriate stop-loss levels based on current volatility.

Bollinger Bands (20-day, 2σ): Three bands (upper, middle, lower) based on SMA ± 2 standard deviations. Band width reflects volatility; price touching bands suggests extreme conditions. Provides models with dynamic volatility-adjusted support and resistance levels.

ADX (14-day): Average Directional Index measures trend strength on a 0-100 scale regardless of direction. Values above 25 indicate a strong trend. Helps models distinguish between trending and range-bound markets for strategy selection.

Volume Indicators

OBV (On-Balance Volume): Cumulative volume indicator that adds volume on up days and subtracts on down days. Divergence between OBV and price suggests potential reversals. Captures buying/selling pressure that may precede price moves.

ADOSC (Chaikin A/D Oscillator): Measures the momentum of the Accumulation/Distribution line. Positive values indicate buying pressure; negative values indicate selling pressure. Provides an additional dimension of institutional flow analysis.

Volume SMA (20-day): Moving average of volume that establishes a baseline for normal trading activity. Spikes above this baseline often precede significant price movements.

Relative Volume: Current volume divided by its 20-day SMA. Values above 1.0 indicate above-average activity, which often accompanies breakouts or reversals. Normalizes volume across different assets for cross-asset comparisons.

3. Models Evaluated

3.1 TimesFM (Google TimesFM 2.5-200M)

Architecture: A decoder-only transformer foundation model pre-trained on over 100 billion real-world time-series data points from Google Trends, Wikipedia pageviews, and public financial datasets. Uses patched time-series tokenization with a 512-point maximum context window and produces both point forecasts and calibrated quantile predictions (P10-P90).

Strengths: State-of-the-art zero-shot accuracy; native probabilistic output; sub-second inference; small memory footprint (0.07-0.11 MB per call).

Weaknesses: Fixed maximum context length (512 points); may smooth over sharp turning points; quantile calibration has not been formally verified on financial data.

Rationale: Selected as the primary foundation model due to Google's extensive pre-training corpus and demonstrated benchmark performance on public forecasting tasks.

3.2 Kronos (NeoQuasar/Kronos-base)

Architecture: A generative transformer model that tokenizes continuous time-series values into a discrete vocabulary, then autoregressively generates future tokens. Uses a custom tokenizer (Kronos-Tokenizer-base) with a 512-token context window and a configurable lookback of 400 data points.

Strengths: True zero-shot generalization; sampling-based architecture could theoretically produce distribution forecasts via repeated sampling; no task-specific tuning required.

Weaknesses: Higher point-forecast error than TimesFM; 15-25x slower inference (4-6 seconds); stochastic output introduces cross-run variance; no native quantile output in the current pipeline configuration.

Rationale: Included as a second foundation model to provide architectural diversity and enable ensemble strategies that combine different model paradigms.

3.3 BaseML (Ridge Regression — Lag Features Only)

Architecture: 30 independent Ridge regression models (one per forecast horizon step h=1..30), each predicting the h-step-ahead return using 5 lagged return features (1, 2, 5, 10, 20-day lags). Hyperparameter α is selected via grid search on the validation split from {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0}.

Rationale: Ridge regression serves as a strong linear baseline. Its L2 regularization prevents coefficient explosion on collinear features, and the lag-only feature set provides a minimal, interpretable model against which more complex approaches can be measured.

3.4 BaseML + Technical Indicators

Same Ridge architecture as BaseML but with the full 25-indicator technical feature set appended to the 5 lag features, yielding ~30 input features per model. The expanded feature set is expected to capture richer market dynamics but introduces risks of overfitting and multicollinearity.

3.5 Weighted Ensemble

Combines TimesFM and Kronos point forecasts using static 50/50 weights. Ensemble averaging reduces prediction variance by exploiting the decorrelation between two architecturally diverse models. When individual models have similar expected error but uncorrelated error patterns, the ensemble's MSE is reduced by a factor proportional to (1 - ρ)/2, where ρ is the error correlation.

3.6 Confidence Ensemble

Dynamically weights TimesFM and Kronos based on each model's validation MAE (inverse-error weighting). Additionally adjusts weights using TimesFM's prediction interval width: narrower intervals → higher TimesFM weight (multiplier range [0.5, 2.0], final weights clipped to [0.05, 0.95]). The rationale is that models demonstrating lower recent validation error deserve proportionally higher influence, and a model that is 'confident' (narrow bands) should be trusted more on that particular forecast window.

4. Trading Strategy

Forecasting alone is insufficient for trading—accurate price predictions must be translated into actionable signals with appropriate confidence thresholds to avoid excessive trading on noisy forecasts.

4.1 Signal Generation Logic

BUY (+1): Generated when the expected 30-day return exceeds +2% (threshold) AND the model's confidence exceeds 40% (min_confidence). For TimesFM, confidence is derived from P(≥+5%) probability; for other models, from prediction interval width relative to the point forecast.

SELL (−1): Generated when the expected 30-day return is below −2% AND confidence exceeds 40%. For TimesFM, this requires P(≥+5%) < 60% (i.e., bearish probability).

HOLD (0): Default signal when expected return is within ±2% or confidence is insufficient. The position is flat (no market exposure).

4.2 Confidence Filtering

The confidence threshold (40%) prevents the strategy from acting on low-conviction forecasts. Without this filter, the model would trade on every window regardless of forecast uncertainty, leading to excessive transaction costs and whipsaw losses in sideways markets. The 2% return threshold further ensures trades are only taken when the expected profit exceeds a meaningful minimum.

5. Backtesting Methodology

Each model's trading signals are backtested across all walk-forward test windows. The backtest applies 10 bps (0.10%) transaction costs per position change and allows both long and short positions. A risk-free rate of 4.0% per annum is assumed for Sharpe and Sortino calculations.

5.1 Performance Metrics

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate): Measures the annualized geometric return: CAGR = (V_end / V_start)^(365.25/N_days) − 1. A positive CAGR indicates the strategy grew capital over the evaluation period. Good: >8% (beating risk-free rate + equity risk premium). Excellent: >15% (consistently outperforming the market).

Sharpe Ratio: Risk-adjusted return: Sharpe = √252 × (mean excess daily return / daily std dev). Measures return per unit of total risk. Good: >0.5. Excellent: >1.0. Negative values indicate the strategy underperformed the risk-free rate.

Sortino Ratio: Similar to Sharpe but penalizes only downside volatility: Sortino = √252 × (mean excess return / downside std dev). More appropriate for strategies with asymmetric return distributions. Good: >1.0. Excellent: >2.0.

Maximum Drawdown: The largest peak-to-trough decline: MDD = min((V_t − V_max) / V_max). Represents the worst-case loss scenario an investor would have experienced. Acceptable: >−20%. Concerning: <−40%. Critical: <−60%.

Win Rate: Percentage of individual 30-day trades that were profitable. Good: >50%. Excellent: >60%. A high win rate with low profit factor suggests many small wins offset by fewer large losses.

Profit Factor: Ratio of gross profits to gross losses: PF = Σ(winning trades) / Σ|losing trades|. Values >1.0 indicate net profitability. Good: >1.5. Excellent: >2.0. Values <1.0 indicate the strategy loses money on aggregate.

9. Regime Analysis

Market regimes were classified using two dimensions: (1) Bull vs. Bear, defined by whether SPY's close is above its 200-day SMA; and (2) High vs. Low Volatility, defined by whether the 20-day rolling standard deviation of returns exceeds its historical median.

Bull Markets: Models generally performed better during bull regimes, as trending markets are more predictable and momentum-based features (SMA, EMA, MACD) align with persistent directional moves. Buy signals in bull markets had higher win rates.

Bear Markets: All models experienced degraded performance during bear markets. Sharp, sudden drawdowns are difficult to forecast using historical patterns, and short signals frequently triggered too late after the initial decline had already occurred.

High Volatility: High-volatility regimes amplified both gains and losses. Models with wider prediction intervals (TimesFM) correctly reduced position sizes via the confidence filter, while models without uncertainty estimates (Kronos, BaseML) suffered larger drawdowns.

Low Volatility: Low-volatility regimes produced the most stable but modest returns. The 2% threshold filter correctly generated HOLD signals during these periods, preventing unnecessary trading on minimal expected moves.

Regime Impact: Performance differences across regimes underscore the importance of adaptive strategy selection. A production system should dynamically adjust thresholds, position sizes, and model weights based on the current regime classification.

Key Observation: Adding the full 25-indicator technical feature set degraded performance on 4 out of 5 assets for both forecasting accuracy (higher MAE) and trading returns (lower CAGR). Only on BTC-USD did the technical features provide a marginal improvement in CAGR (+0.20% vs. -9.75%).

Volume Indicator Analysis

The volume-based indicators (OBV, ADOSC, Volume SMA, Relative Volume) were expected to capture institutional flow dynamics and confirm price movements. However, their inclusion increased overall MAE, suggesting several contributing factors:

Overfitting: With only ~440-500 training samples per window and 30+ features, the model's effective degrees of freedom approach the sample size, increasing the risk of fitting noise rather than signal. Ridge regularization mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.

Multicollinearity: Volume indicators are correlated with price-based indicators (e.g., OBV trends with price trends, ADOSC correlates with momentum). This multicollinearity inflates coefficient variance in the Ridge model, degrading generalization.

Noisy Features: Volume data is inherently noisier than price data due to institutional block trades, options expiration flows, and index rebalancing events. These noise sources are regime-dependent and difficult to model with linear features.

11. Forecast Accuracy vs. Trading Performance

A critical finding of this study is that superior forecast accuracy does not automatically translate into superior investment returns. This disconnect arises from several factors observed in the results:

Signal Thresholds: TimesFM achieved the lowest MAE on most assets but its confidence filtering (requiring P(≥+5%) ≥ 40%) often generated HOLD signals, resulting in 0% CAGR on MSFT. Meanwhile, BaseML's less accurate but more decisive signals captured NVDA's +67% rally.

Timing and Directionality: A model can have low MAE while consistently predicting the wrong direction on critical turning points. Directional accuracy is more relevant for trading than absolute error, but even 52-54% DA generates marginal edge after costs.

Position Sizing: All models used binary ±1/0 position sizing. A more sophisticated approach would scale position size with forecast confidence and expected return magnitude, potentially improving risk-adjusted returns.

Trade Frequency: TimesFM's stringent confidence filter resulted in very few active trades (win rate = 0% on some assets, meaning no trades were taken). This capital inefficiency means the strategy earned no returns despite having the best model.

Market Dynamics: In strongly trending markets (NVDA 2021-2024), even moderate forecasting ability produces large returns via trend-following. In mean-reverting markets, the same approach generates whipsaw losses.

12. Experiment Tracking

All experiment runs are logged into a SQLite database (data/experiments.db) to ensure full reproducibility and facilitate comparative analysis across experiments.

Metadata Stored: Each record includes: timestamp, model version, hyperparameters (horizon, validation days, context length, transaction costs, risk-free rate), dataset specification (ticker, period, dry-run flag), and all computed metrics (CAGR, Sharpe, Sortino, Max Drawdown, Win Rate, Profit Factor, MAE, RMSE, Directional Accuracy).

Reproducibility Benefits: The database enables: (1) comparison of 3y vs. 5y experiments to validate that extended history improves coverage; (2) tracking model performance over time as new data becomes available; (3) auditing of specific run configurations to understand anomalous results; (4) automated reporting from structured data rather than ad-hoc analysis.

13. Limitations

Statistical Significance: With ~25 non-overlapping windows per equity asset, the effective sample size for trading performance metrics is small. Sharpe ratio estimates from 25 observations have wide confidence intervals (±0.4 at 95% CI).

Survivorship Bias: All five test assets are among the largest and most liquid in their respective markets. Results may not generalize to small-cap equities, emerging market assets, or less liquid instruments.

Transaction Cost Model: The 10 bps per-trade cost assumption is conservative for institutional equity trading but may underestimate costs for BTC-USD (where spreads, slippage, and exchange fees can exceed 20-50 bps).

Look-Ahead in Regime Classification: The 200-day SMA used for bull/bear classification uses the full historical series, which is available at each walk-forward step. However, the SMA itself is a lagging indicator and may not accurately reflect regime transitions in real-time.

Fixed Signal Thresholds: The 2% return threshold and 40% confidence minimum were not optimized and may not be appropriate for all assets or market conditions. These parameters should be treated as hyperparameters subject to cross-validation.

Single Ridge Model: Only Ridge regression was evaluated as the traditional ML baseline. More flexible models (XGBoost, LightGBM, neural networks) may better capture non-linear feature interactions.

Metric Anomalies: Several assets show 0.0% CAGR / 0.0% win rate for TimesFM—indicating the model generated no active trades due to confidence filtering. While this prevents losses, it also prevents any capital deployment.

14. Future Improvements

Probabilistic Forecasting: Formally evaluate TimesFM's quantile calibration using pinball loss and coverage tests. Integrate prediction intervals into position sizing: larger positions when intervals are narrow, smaller when uncertainty is high.

Dynamic Ensembles: Replace static and validation-MAE-based weighting with regime-adaptive ensemble methods that adjust model weights based on recent rolling performance across different market conditions.

Hyperparameter Optimization: Use Bayesian optimization (Optuna) to jointly optimize signal thresholds, confidence minimums, Ridge alpha, and ensemble weights within the walk-forward framework.
